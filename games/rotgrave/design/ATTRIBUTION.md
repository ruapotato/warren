## Where this project came from

ROTGRAVE was written for Godot and has been ported to **Warren**, the
author's own engine. The bodies came across with it, out of the author's
own **Rhabdos** project: Rhabdos's `RHFIG2` figure container is a private
format and Warren has no business learning to read it, so the figures are
converted **once, offline** by `tools/rhfig_to_gltf.py` and what is in
this repository is plain glTF that any tool can open. Nothing of
Rhabdos's voxel engine, world generation or game logic is used here.

## Humanoid bodies — CC0 1.0

The survivor and eight of the ten undead species are **MakeHuman** meshes,
built through the **MPFB2** Blender add-on: base mesh, morph targets,
skeleton, clothing and hair are all released **CC0** by the MakeHuman
project and its community-pack authors.

- **MakeHuman** / **MPFB2** — http://www.makehumancommunity.org — CC0 1.0.
- Community assets in the shipped builds: `toigo_fisherman_sweater`,
  `rehmanpolanski_viking_tunic`, `rehmanpolanski_viking_pants`,
  `rehmanpolanski_viking_boots`, `donitz_monk_robe`,
  `grinsegold_corinthian_helmet`, `elvs_unkempt_french_braid`,
  `rehmanpolanski_hair_bun_brown`, `cortu_short_messy_hair`,
  `sonntag78_junglebook_hair`.

They were authored as `<name>.json` (a manifest: bones, surfaces,
textures) and `.bin` (the geometry), and are committed here as
`assets/figures/<name>.glb` — one binary glTF each, textures embedded,
rigged to the same nineteen bones. `assets/figures/figures.json` records
each one's height, which glTF has nowhere to put and the game needs.
The loader is `rotgrave/figures.py`.

The player is `hero.glb`: the **female build**, `f_bare`, worn with
`f_wear_leathers`, `f_wear_breeches`, `f_wear_boots` and the
`f_hair_braid01` haircut, composed at conversion time.

The converter works out MakeHuman's clothing delete groups itself rather
than reading them, since they did not survive the original export — see
`hidden_skin()` for why the body underneath has to go and how it is
found.

## Humanoid animation — CC0 1.0

Every person in this game — survivor and risen alike — walks, runs,
crouches, reloads, punches and dies on **recorded motion**, retargeted at
runtime onto the MakeHuman skeleton.

- **Quaternius** — *Universal Animation Library (Standard)* —
  https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html — CC0 1.0.
  Obtained from https://opengameart.org/content/universal-animation-library
- Shipped as `assets/anim/quaternius_ual.glb` (46 clips on a standard
  Rigify humanoid). The pack's own licence text is beside it.

  **Not yet wired up in the Warren port** — the figures stand still. The
  retargeter is the next piece of the move.

The library's rig is not this game's rig. The clips are retargeted bone by
bone **by role**, in world space, taking rotations only — see
the deleted Godot version's `clips.gd` for the method and `clipbank.gd`
for the table they were baked into at load. Both are still to be ported.

## Sound effects — the author's own

Two sets, both generated, nothing authored as a binary:

- `tools/gen_sfx.py` — every weapon, impact, creature voice, machine and
  round cue in this game. Regenerate with
  `python3 tools/gen_sfx.py --out assets/sfx`.
- The fantasy-game set carried over from Rhabdos (footsteps, doors, UI),
  originally generated by that project's `tools/generate_sfx.py`.

## Textures — generated

No texture in this game is a photograph or a painted asset.

- `tools/gen_world_tex.py` — the town's 27 materials, each a tileable
  albedo, a normal map derived from the same heightfield that shaped it,
  and an ORM (occlusion/roughness/metalness). The nine the town actually
  lays are committed under `assets/world`; `--all` makes the rest.
- `tools/gen_textures.py` — the bodies' skin, eyes, hair, brows and cloth.
  The upstream figure pipeline's textures are build output and were never
  committed, so these are synthesised to match each surface's recorded
  mean colour, which keeps every in-game tint landing where it was aimed.
  The eyes are the interesting one: the iris is placed by reading the
  eyeball's own geometry and UVs out of the `.bin`.

## The portal and grapple extension

In the Godot version these were a GDExtension written in C against the
engine's public C ABI. Warren is the author's own engine, so they are
ordinary engine code here and vendor nothing.
