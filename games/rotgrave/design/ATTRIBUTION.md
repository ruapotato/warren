## Where this project came from

ROTGRAVE is built on assets and two pieces of machinery from the author's
own **Rhabdos** project (`~/Rhabdos`): the figure format and its loader,
and the runtime animation retargeter. Both are carried over with their
original documentation intact. Nothing of Rhabdos's voxel engine, world
generation or game logic is used here.

## Humanoid bodies — CC0 1.0

The survivor and eight of the ten undead species are **MakeHuman** meshes,
built through the **MPFB2** Blender add-on: base mesh, morph targets,
skeleton, clothing and hair are all released **CC0** by the MakeHuman
project and its community-pack authors.

- **MakeHuman** / **MPFB2** — http://www.makehumancommunity.org — CC0 1.0.
- Community assets in the shipped builds: `toigo_fisherman_sweater`,
  `rehmanpolanski_viking_tunic`, `rehmanpolanski_viking_pants`,
  `rehmanpolanski_viking_boots`, `donitz_monk_robe`,
  `grinsegold_corinthian_helmet`, `elvs_unkempt_french_braid`,
  `rehmanpolanski_hair_bun_brown`, `cortu_short_messy_hair`,
  `sonntag78_junglebook_hair`.

They arrive as `godot/assets/body/<name>.json` (a manifest: bones,
surfaces, textures) and `.bin` (the geometry). The loader is
`godot/actors/figure.gd`.

The player is the **female build**, `f_bare` plus per-piece clothing.

## Humanoid animation — CC0 1.0

Every person in this game — survivor and risen alike — walks, runs,
crouches, reloads, punches and dies on **recorded motion**, retargeted at
runtime onto the MakeHuman skeleton.

- **Quaternius** — *Universal Animation Library (Standard)* —
  https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html — CC0 1.0.
  Obtained from https://opengameart.org/content/universal-animation-library
- Shipped as `godot/assets/anim/quaternius_ual.glb` (46 clips on a standard
  Rigify humanoid). The pack's own licence text is beside it at
  `godot/assets/anim/LICENSE_quaternius.txt`.

The library's rig is not this game's rig. The clips are retargeted bone by
bone **by role**, in world space, taking rotations only — see
`godot/actors/clips.gd` for the method and `godot/actors/clipbank.gd` for
the table they are baked into at load.

## Music — GPL-3.0

`godot/assets/audio/music/` — tracks from **The Legend of Tux** (same
author), remixed to mono 32 kHz. GPL-3.0.

## Sound effects — the author's own

Two sets, both generated, nothing authored as a binary:

- `tools/gen_sfx.py` — every weapon, impact, creature voice, machine and
  round cue in this game. Regenerate with
  `python3 tools/gen_sfx.py --out godot/assets/audio/sfx`.
- The fantasy-game set carried over from Rhabdos (footsteps, doors, UI),
  originally generated by that project's `tools/generate_sfx.py`.

## Textures — generated

No texture in this game is a photograph or a painted asset.

- `tools/gen_world_tex.py` — the town's 26 materials, each an albedo, a
  derived normal map and a roughness map, all tileable.
- `tools/gen_textures.py` — the bodies' skin, eyes, hair, brows and cloth.
  The upstream figure pipeline's textures are build output and were never
  committed, so these are synthesised to match each surface's recorded
  mean colour, which keeps every in-game tint landing where it was aimed.
  The eyes are the interesting one: the iris is placed by reading the
  eyeball's own geometry and UVs out of the `.bin`.

## The portal and grapple extension — the author's own

`plugin/` is a GDExtension written in C against Godot's public C ABI. It
vendors exactly one third-party file:

- `plugin/src/gdextension_interface.h` — the engine's own C ABI header,
  MIT, shipped with Godot and copied unmodified.

It deliberately does **not** use godot-cpp; the engine bindings it needs
are generated from this build's `extension_api.json` by
`plugin/tools/genbinds.py`.
